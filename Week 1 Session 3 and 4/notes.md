# Week 1 Session 3 & 4: Strategic Building Blocks — Mission to Tactic

---

## 1. RECAP

* A **policy** is a standing guideline — a boundary, not a script.
* A **program** is a time-bound set of activities aimed at one goal.
* A **procedure** is a fixed, repeatable, step-by-step method.

---

## 2. What Is a Budget?

* A plan expressed in numbers: how much money will be spent, on what, and by when.
* A budget turns good intentions into a resource commitment; it forces you to choose, because money is always limited.

### Example

> *“₹8,000 for ingredients, ₹3,000 for gas and packaging, ₹2,000 for a helper this month, no more.”*

---

## 3. What Is a Tactic?

* A short-term, specific move made to gain an immediate advantage.
* If a program is a whole campaign, a tactic is one small, quick action inside it — often decided in the moment, not planned months ahead.

### Example

> *“Free chutney sachet for the first 20 customers this Friday”* — a quick, small nudge, not a big plan.

---

## 4. His Budget & Tactics

* **Budget**: ₹6,000/month on vegetables, ₹2,500 on gas and packaging, ₹1,500 set aside for a helper on weekends — ₹10,000 total, tracked weekly.
* **Tactic**: Noticing a slow Tuesday afternoon, Mohit/Aditya hands out a free chutney sachet to the next 20 customers — a quick, unplanned nudge.

## 5. What Is a Mission?

* A **mission** is the reason a business exists — broader than any single goal, and stable even as objectives, budgets and tactics change year to year.

### Amul's Mission, in Spirit

> *“To give India's dairy farmers fair, remunerative returns for their milk.”*
> That idea hasn't changed since 1946 — even as the specific objectives, budgets and products around it changed constantly.

---

## 6. His Mission Statement

> *“To bring fresh, hygienic, affordable momos to every student in this city, without them ever having to choose between taste and their budget.”*

* **Notice**: This mission doesn't mention 100 plates a day, or gloves, or Friday combos. It's bigger and slower-moving than any of those — everything else we've written for Bono serves this one idea.

> *“Priya's Momos” is a fictional business created for classroom illustration only.*

## 7. A Story to Start: Why a Farmers' Cooperative Needed a Mission

* **1946, Anand, Gujarat**: A group of local dairy farmers, tired of unfair middlemen, form a cooperative. No fancy office, no big budget — just farmers and milk.
* **The Mission That Held It Together**: Give farmers themselves fair, remunerative returns for their milk — not maximize profit for a few owners. Every later decision traced back to that one idea.
* **Today**: That cooperative is one of India's largest food brands, still owned by its farmer-members. A clear mission, followed for decades, built an empire no single budget or tactic ever could alone.

> *History based on widely documented accounts of Amul's founding (1946) as a farmers' cooperative.*

---

## 8. A Useful Distinction: Mission, Goal & Objectives

| Mission                          | Goal                                                        | Objectives                                      |
| :------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| Why the business exists          | What the business aims/wants to achieve overall (long-term) | What it's trying to achieve, right now          |
| Broad, stable, rarely changes    | Medium to long-term, semi-broad, updated periodically       | Specific, measurable, changes often             |
| *“Fair returns for farmers”* | Expand the milk procurement and processing network          | *“Increase milk procurement 10% this year”* |

---

## 9. The Full Stack: From Mission to Tactic

* **Mission**: Why we exist
* **Goal**: What we want to achieve over the long term
* **Objectives**: What we're specifically trying to achieve
* **Strategy**: The overarching direction to win
* **Policy**: The standing guidelines we follow
* **Program**: The time-bound activities we run
* **Procedure**: The exact steps we repeat
* **Budget**: The numbers behind it all
* **Tactic**: The quick moves we make in the moment

---

## 10. Back to Bonsho's Momos: His Complete Picture, All Nine Blocks

* **Mission**: Fresh, hygienic, affordable momos for every student
* **Goal / Strategy**: Become the most popular student momo spot in the area / Become the go-to campus momo stall
* **Objective**: 100 plates/day within 3 months
* **Policy**: Only fresh vegetables, never leftovers
* **Program**: Friday Combo Week
* **Procedure**: 5-step plating process, every order
* **Budget**: ₹10,000/month, tracked weekly
* **Tactic**: Free chutney for first 20 on slow days

---

## 11. A Common Mistake: Why Some Founders Get This Wrong

1. **Writing an inspiring mission, then never translating it into a real objective or budget**.
2. **Setting strict procedures and policies with no mission behind them** — staff follow rules without understanding why.
3. **Running exciting tactics and programs that quietly contradict the stated mission**.
4. **The Fix**: Everything below the mission should trace back up to it, cleanly.

---

## 12. Summary: Key Takeaways from Today

* A **budget** is a plan expressed in numbers — it forces real trade-offs.
* A **tactic** is a quick, short-term move — smaller and faster than a program.
* A **mission** is the reason a business exists — broad and stable, unlike an objective.
* All **nine building blocks** — mission, goals, objectives, strategy, policy, program, procedure, budget, tactic — should trace back to one mission.
