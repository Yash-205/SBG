## 1. What Are Resources? (Resources, Capability, Competency)

* **Resource**: A source of supply, support, or available means to function or reach a goal.
* **Capability**: The organization’s ability to use its resources to perform a particular activity effectively.
* **Competency**: The organization’s proven ability to perform an activity better than competitors or create superior value.

---

## 2.Core Concept: Four Kinds of Resources

* **Physical**: Buildings, equipment, inventory.
* **Financial**: Cash, credit, access to capital.
* **Human**: People and their skills.
* **Intangible**: Brand, reputation, patents, culture.

## 5. Why This Matters for a Founder

1. **Investors fund resources, but they bet on competencies** — what you can actually do with what you're given.
2. **Two businesses can have identical resources and completely different outcomes**, based on competence.
3. **Knowing your weakest resource tells you exactly what to fundraise, hire, or build for next**.

## 6. Summary: Key Takeaways from Today

* Four resource types: **physical, financial, human, and intangible**.
* Resources are things you have; competencies are what you can do with them.
* Sun Tzu priced out an army's resources 2,500 years ago — and warned that even strong resources drain if not actively protected.
* Investors fund resources, but they bet on competencies.

## 7. Term of the Day: Apostatic Selection

* **Apostatic Selection**: In evolutionary biology, predators search for the common type of prey, allowing rare and uncommon variants to survive.
* In business: In crowded markets where competitors copy each other, adopting an unconventional, distinct business model helps a company thrive by staying outside the direct crosshairs of dominant incumbents.

## 8. Unit 1 Complete: A Quick Master Recap

### Recap 1: Building Blocks & Management Functions

* **The Nine Blocks**: Mission (why we exist), Goal (long-term aim), Objectives (specific targets), Strategy (direction to win), Policy (standing guidelines), Program (time-bound activities), Procedure (exact steps), Budget (numbers behind it), Tactic (quick moves).
* **Strategic Management Process**: Planning, Decision-Making, Formulating, Evaluating.

### The Ten Principles of Strategy

* **Principles 1–5 (Setting the Direction)**:
  1. Strategy is about the future (decided today, played out over years).
  2. Strategy needs trade-offs (you cannot be everything to everyone).
  3. Strategy must fit the environment (what works depends on the world outside).
  4. Strategy must fit your capabilities (ambition without resources is a wish).
  5. Strategy aims for lasting advantage (not just one good quarter).
* **Principles 6–10 (Making It Real)**:
  6. Top management sets it, everyone implements it (needs buy-in at every level).
  7. Strategy always involves risk (no guaranteed outcomes, ever).
  8. Strategy must stay flexible (rigid plans break when conditions change).
  9. Strategy is only as good as its execution (a brilliant plan, poorly run, still fails).
  10. Strategy needs continuous evaluation (check it's working; correct course early).

### Recap 2: Strategy Levels (Corporate, SBU, Functional)

* **Corporate**: Which businesses to be in. *Reliance deciding to enter telecom with Jio.*
* **SBU (Business Unit)**: How to win within one business. *Jio's pricing war within telecom specifically.*
* **Functional**: How each department executes. *Jio's network rollout, marketing, and pricing teams each playing their part.*

### Recap 3: PESTEL & Environmental Scanning

* **PESTEL**: Political · Economic · Social · Technological · Environmental · Legal.
* *Nokia's decline*: A technological shift (touchscreen smartphones) it scanned for too slowly.
* *Demonetisation (2016) and GST (2017)*: Two real Economic and Legal shocks that reshaped Indian business overnight.

### Recap 4: Porter's Five Forces

* **Forces**: Threat of New Entrants · Supplier Power · Buyer Power · Threat of Substitutes · Rivalry.
* *Patanjali* entered FMCG from a level HUL and Colgate had overlooked entirely — home remedies and Ayurveda — and turned that blind spot into real brand-level rivalry within a few years.

### Recap 5: SWOT Analysis

* **Internal**: Strengths and Weaknesses. What Boncho’s Momos controls: her recipe, her costs, her hygiene standards.
* **External**: Opportunities and Threats. What Boncho’s Momos does not control: a new competitor opening nearby, a festival boosting footfall.

### Recap 6: Value Chain & Cost Analysis

* Primary activities create the product; support activities make the primary ones possible.
* *Hero Cycles* built its cost advantage through supplier relationships and logistics, not just factory efficiency.
* *Sun Tzu's economics of war*: “The skillful soldier forages on the enemy.” A lean cost structure isn't a limitation, it's a weapon.

### Recap 8: The Four Resource Types

* **Physical**: Warehouses, delivery fleets.
* **Financial**: Cash reserves, ability to raise capital.
* **Human**: Talent, leadership, culture.
* **Intangible**: Brand trust, patents, data.
