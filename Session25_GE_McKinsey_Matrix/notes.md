# Week 7 Session 25: The GE / McKinsey Nine-Cell Matrix

---

## 1. Why This Tool Exists: The Problem BCG Couldn't Solve

* Two real businesses can have identical growth and share numbers, and still be nothing alike.
* A slow-growing category can still be genuinely attractive: *high margins, few competitors, loyal customers, low regulatory risk*.
* BCG's two axes can't capture that nuance.
* **General Electric**, working with **McKinsey & Company** in the early 1970s, built a richer version:
  * Instead of two numbers, it uses **two composite judgments**: **Industry Attractiveness** and **Business Strength**, each built from multiple real factors.
  * GE's matrix was built specifically to let managers **weigh in strategic judgment**, not just plug in two numbers.

---

## 2. Core Concept: Two Composite Axes, Not Two Numbers

| Axis | Factors Considered |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Industry Attractiveness** *(Vertical Axis)* | Market size, market growth rate, competitive intensity, profit margins, technology requirements, regulatory environment. |
| **Business Strength / Competitive Position** *(Horizontal Axis)* | Market share, brand strength, cost position, distribution reach, technical capability, management quality. |

---

## 3. The Nine Cells and Three Strategic Zones

| Industry Attractiveness \ Business Strength | Strong Business | Average Business | Weak Business |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Industry Attractiveness** | 🟢 **Invest / Grow** | 🟢 **Selective Investment** | 🟡 **Build / Select** |
| **Medium Industry Attractiveness** | 🟢 **Selective Growth** | 🟡 **Hold** | 🟠 **Harvest** |
| **Low Industry Attractiveness** | 🟡 **Protect / Harvest** | 🟠 **Harvest** | 🔴 **Exit / Divest** |

### The Three Broad Zones:
1. **Invest / Grow (Top-Left)**: Strong on both axes $\rightarrow$ Fund aggressively; this is where the company's future is being built.
2. **Selective Growth / Hold (Diagonal)**: Mixed strengths $\rightarrow$ Invest carefully; fix specific weaknesses before committing more capital.
3. **Harvest / Divest (Bottom-Right)**: Weak on both axes $\rightarrow$ Minimise new investment, extract remaining cash flow, or exit entirely.

* **Founded 1897**: From locks and safes to one of India's most diversified conglomerates (consumer products, real estate, agrochemicals, appliances).

| SBU / Business | GE Matrix Position | Strategic Analysis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 5. How the Matrix Is Actually Built: Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Select Factors & Assign Weights
Assign weights to each factor such that all weights on an axis sum to $100\%$ (or $1.0$).

| Industry Attractiveness Factors | Weight | Business Strength Factors | Weight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Market Growth Rate | 40% | Relative Market Share | 35% |
| Industry Profit Margins | 30% | Brand Reputation | 30% |
| Market Size | 20% | Technological Capabilities | 20% |
| Barriers to Entry | 10% | Profit Margins | 15% |
| **Total** | **100%** | **Total** | **100%** |

### Step 2: Rate Each Factor (1 to 5 Scale)
* **Rating Scale**: Rate each factor from $1$ (Poor / Weak) to $5$ (Excellent / Strong).
* Multiply each rating by its weight and sum them up to get a composite score between $1.0$ and $5.0$.

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{3.67 - 5.00} &\longrightarrow \textbf{High} \\
\mathbf{2.34 - 3.66} &\longrightarrow \textbf{Medium} \\
\mathbf{1.00 - 2.33} &\longrightarrow \textbf{Low}
\end{aligned}$$

### Step 3: Example Evaluation Table

| SBU / Product Line | Industry Attractiveness Score | Attractiveness Category | Business Strength Score | Strength Category | Strategic Position |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| 🏎️ **Electric Vehicles (EV)** | 4.6 | High | 2.1 | Low | **Selective Investment** |
| 📱 **Smartphones** | 3.5 | Medium | 4.2 | High | **Protect / Hold** |
| 📷 **Digital Cameras** | 1.5 | Low | 1.8 | Low | **Divest** |

## 6. Comparing the Two Portfolio Tools

| Dimension | BCG Matrix (1970) | GE / McKinsey Matrix (1970s) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Axes Used** | 2 objective metrics (*Market Growth Rate, Relative Market Share*) | 2 composite multi-factor judgments (*Industry Attractiveness, Business Strength*) |
| **Grid Size** | 4 quadrants ($2 \times 2$) | 9 cells ($3 \times 3$) |
| **Key Advantage** | Fast, simple, objective, easy to defend | Nuanced, comprehensive, captures real-world qualitative complexities |
| **Key Limitation** | Oversimplifies; ignores profitability, barriers, and brand power | Subjective; weights and scores can be biased or gamed by managers |

---

## 7. A Common Mistake: The Weights Are Never Neutral

* Whoever picks the factors and assigns the weights is making a strategic argument, whether they admit it or not.
* A manager defending a pet project can quietly weight the factors that make it look attractive.
* **Principle**: The tool is only as honest as the people scoring it.

---

## 8. From the Art of War: Sun Tzu, Chapter III

> *“If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles.”*

* **Industry Attractiveness** is knowing the enemy and the terrain.
* **Business Strength** is knowing yourself.
* The GE Matrix simply forces both judgments to be made honestly, side by side.

## 9. Term of the Day: The Poison Pill Effect

* **Definition**: A defensive mechanism used by a target company's board to prevent or deter hostile takeovers.
* **Mechanism (Shareholder Rights Plan)**: Allows existing shareholders (excluding the hostile bidder) to purchase additional shares at a deep discount, drastically diluting the acquirer's ownership stake and making the takeover financially prohibitive.
* **Strategic Context**: Often used to force an unwanted acquirer to negotiate with the board or buy time to seek a friendly buyer (*White Knight*).

## 10. Connecting Back: Feeding Into Grand Strategy

* The GE Matrix doesn't replace anything you've learned; it organizes it.
* A business landing in **Invest/Grow** is a primary candidate for **Growth Strategies** (Ansoff Matrix, Market Penetration, Integration).
* A business landing in **Harvest/Divest** is a clear candidate for **Retrenchment Strategies**.
* The matrix tells you **where to look**; Ansoff and the grand strategies tell you **what to do**.

---

## 11. Key Takeaways from Today

* The GE / McKinsey Matrix uses two composite axes: **Industry Attractiveness** and **Business Strength**, built from multiple weighted factors.
* It was built to capture nuance that BCG's simpler two-axis approach misses.
* The nine cells collapse into three strategic zones: **Invest/Grow, Selective Growth, and Harvest/Divest**.
* Because weights and scores rely on judgment, the tool is only as honest as the people using it.
