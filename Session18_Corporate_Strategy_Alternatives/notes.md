# Week 5 Session 18: Corporate Strategy Alternatives

---

## 3. Core Concept: The Four Grand Strategy Alternatives

| Alternative            | Core Focus                                  |
| :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------ |
| **Stability**    | Keep doing what's working                   |
| **Growth**       | Expand scale or scope                       |
| **Retrenchment** | Shrink, exit, or turn around                |
| **Combination**  | Different moves in different units, at once |

---

## 4. Alternative 1 of 4: Stability — The Underrated Choice

* **Definition**: Stability means continuing on the same path, same products, same markets, same scale, deliberately. It is not the same as doing nothing.
* **When Chosen**:
  * Chosen when the environment is stable.
  * The current strategy is genuinely working.
  * When rapid growth would strain resources, quality, or culture past their limits.
* **Real-World Example**:
  * Many family-run Indian sweets and namkeen brands hold a single city or region deliberately for decades, protecting quality and margins over chasing national scale.

---

## 5. Alternative 2 of 4: Growth — Expanding Scale or Scope

* **Definition**: Growth means increasing sales, market share, assets, or the number of markets served.
* **Real-World Example (Reliance Jio, 2016)**:
  * Reliance Jio's 2016 launch: a deliberate, aggressive growth bet, built on the belief that Indian data consumption was about to explode.
  * It was right, and it reshaped an entire industry.

---

## 6. Alternative 3 of 4: Retrenchment — When Shrinking Is the Strategy

* **Definition**: Retrenchment means reducing scale, exiting a business, or restructuring debt.
* **When Chosen**: Chosen when a business is losing money, losing relevance, or losing the ability to compete.
* **Real-World Example (Jet Airways, 2019)**:
  * Once India's leading full-service airline, Jet Airways grounded its entire fleet in April 2019 under debt it could no longer service.
  * The retrenchment signal, cutting routes and staff, had come far too late to save the business.
  * **The Lesson**: The lesson isn't that retrenchment is shameful, it's that delaying it usually makes it worse.

---

## 7. From the Art of War: Sun Tzu, Chapter III

> *“If equally matched, we can offer battle... if we are ignorant both of the enemy and of ourselves, we are certain to be in peril... if we know neither, we are sure to be defeated in every battle.”*

* Choosing between growth, stability, and retrenchment is exactly this kind of self-knowledge.
* Jet Airways kept fighting a battle its own finances could no longer support.

---

## 8. Alternative 4 of 4: Combination — All at Once, in Different Places

* Large, diversified companies rarely pick just one alternative.
* Different business units can pursue growth, stability, or retrenchment simultaneously, under one corporate umbrella.
* **Real-World Example (Tata Group, 2020s)**:
  * While aggressively growing Tata Digital and Air India's turnaround, the Group has also quietly divested or restructured older, underperforming units elsewhere in its portfolio.
  * Growth and retrenchment, running side by side, in the same company.

---

## 9. Class Discussion: Which Alternative for Boncho's Momos?

* **Scenario**: A cloud kitchen just opened two streets away, undercutting Boncho's prices by 15%.
* **Discussion Question**: Would you recommend Stability, Growth, Retrenchment, or Combination, and why?
* *There is no single correct answer — defend your reasoning.*

---

## 10. What Actually Drives the Choice?

1. **Industry Attractiveness**: Is this category worth being in at all?
2. **Competitive Position**: Are we strong or weak relative to rivals here?
3. **Resource Availability**: Can we actually fund the option we prefer?
4. **Risk Appetite**: Of the leadership and the board.

---

## 12. A Common Mistake: Growth Is Not Always the Right Answer

* Many founders and managers treat growth as the default, and everything else as failure.
* But a business that grows into a weak position — more revenue, worse margins, thinner resources — hasn't actually gotten stronger.
* It's just bigger and more fragile.

---

## 13. Key Takeaways from Today

* There are four grand strategy alternatives: **Stability, Growth, Retrenchment, and Combination** — not just growth.
* Retrenchment chosen early is a strategy; retrenchment forced late, like Jet Airways, is a collapse.
* Large diversified companies routinely combine different alternatives across different business units.
* The choice depends on industry attractiveness, competitive position, resources, and risk appetite — everything Unit 1 taught you to assess.

> **Growth is a choice, not a default.**
