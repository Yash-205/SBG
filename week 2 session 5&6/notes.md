## 2. One Company, Two Very Different Kinds of Decisions (Titan & HMT)

* **1984**: Tata Group and a Tamil Nadu state agency launch **Titan** to sell quartz watches — a bold bet against HMT's decades-old mechanical-watch dominance.
* **1994**: Titan makes an even bigger bet: entering an entirely different industry, jewellery, by launching **Tanishq** — competing against family jewellers nobody had ever organized into a modern brand.
* **Meanwhile, every single day**: Titan also enforces small, standing guidelines — quality checks, purity certification, retail store standards — that never make headlines but never stop either.

> *History based on widely documented accounts of Titan Company (founded 1984) and Tanishq (launched 1994).*

---

## 3. What Is Strategy, Really?

* A **strategy** is a big, hard-to-reverse choice about where to compete and how to win there — not a rule for handling a routine situation.

### Titan's Two Strategic Bets

* Entering watches in 1984, and entering jewellery in 1994, were both strategies — they picked a whole new market to compete in, committed years of resources, and couldn't be undone overnight.

---

## 4. Policy vs. Strategy: The Core Difference

| Policy                                     | Strategy                                       |
| :----------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| A standing guideline for routine decisions | A one-time, high-stakes choice about direction |
| Easy to reverse or adjust                  | Hard and costly to reverse once made           |
| *“Only fresh ingredients, always”*     | *“Enter the jewellery business”*           |

> *Based on: A. Nag, Strategic Management: Analysis, Implementation and Control, Ch. 4.*

---

## 5. Why the Confusion?

* Both sound like “direction.” Both get written down. Both guide decisions.
* So students — and even managers — often use the words interchangeably.
* But mixing them up has a real cost:
  * Treating a strategic decision like a routine policy means **under-thinking it**.
  * Treating a policy like a strategy means **over-analyzing something that should just be a standing rule**.

---

## 6. Three Questions to Tell Them Apart

1. **Scope**: Does this affect the whole direction of the business, or just one routine situation?
2. **Reversibility**: Could we undo this next month without much cost — or are we now committed for years?
3. **Frequency**: Will we make this exact call again and again, or is this a one-time turning point?

---

## 7. Back to Titan: Which One Was Which?

* Entering the watch market (1984) $\rightarrow$ **STRATEGY**
* Launching Tanishq jewellery (1994) $\rightarrow$ **STRATEGY**
* Certifying gold purity in every store, every day $\rightarrow$ **POLICY**

---

## 8. Back to Priya's Momos: Policy or Strategy?

* Only using fresh vegetables, every single day $\rightarrow$ **POLICY**
* Deciding to shift from a cart to a full cloud kitchen $\rightarrow$ **STRATEGY**
* Deciding to target office workers instead of just students $\rightarrow$ **STRATEGY**

> *“Priya's Momos” is a fictional business created for classroom illustration only.*

---

## 9. Class Discussion: Policy or Strategy? You Decide

* *“We will expand our tuition centre to two new cities next year.”* $\rightarrow$ **Strategy** (Major geographic bet, capital commitment).
* *“All refunds must be processed within 48 hours.”* $\rightarrow$ **Policy** (Standing operational boundary).
* *“We are shutting our physical store and going online-only.”* $\rightarrow$ **Strategy** (Fundamental business model pivot).

---

## 10. Why This Distinction Matters for Entrepreneurs

1. Treating a strategic bet like routine policy means committing years of resources without enough thought.
2. Treating a routine policy like a strategic decision wastes time that should go into execution.
3. Investors and co-founders will ask you to defend your strategy — not your policies.
4. Knowing which kind of decision you're making tells you how much analysis it actually deserves.

---

## 11. Ten Principles · Part 1: Principles 1–5 (Setting the Direction)

1. **Is about the future**: Decided today, played out over years.
2. **Needs trade-offs**: You cannot be everything to everyone.
3. **Must fit the environment**: What works depends on the world outside.
4. **Must fit your capabilities**: Ambition without resources is a wish.
5. **Aims for lasting advantage**: Not just one good quarter.
6. **Top management sets it, everyone implements it**: Strategy needs buy-in at every level.
7. **Always involves risk**: No guaranteed outcomes, ever.
8. **Must stay flexible**: Rigid plans break when conditions change.
9. **Strategy is only as good as its execution**: A brilliant plan, poorly run, still fails.
10. **Strategy needs continuous evaluation**: Check it's working; correct course early.

---

## 13. Why These Principles Matter for You as a Founder

1. They give you a checklist — something to test your own idea against before committing.
2. They explain why some ideas that sound great on paper still fail in the real world.
3. They remind you that strategy is a discipline, not a single moment of inspiration.

---

## 14. Summary

* Good strategy always involves trade-offs — deliberately choosing what NOT to do.
* Strategy must fit both the outside environment and your own real capabilities.
* Execution matters as much as the plan itself — often more.
* These same ten principles apply whether you're Titan or a single momo cart.

---

## 15. Term of the Day: Schwerpunkt & Achilles' Heel

* **Schwerpunkt**: Focal Point or Center of Gravity (the decisive point where an organization concentrates force).
* **Achilles' Heel**: Similarly, all organizations have an Achilles heel — a vulnerable single point of failure.
