# Week 2 Session 7: The Strategic Management Model — Four Stages

---

## 1. The Strategic Management Model (Four Stages)

1. **Planning**: Look outward and inward before deciding anything.
2. **Formulation**: Design the actual strategy.
3. **Implementation**: Put the strategy into action.
4. **Evaluation**: Check if it's working, and adjust.

> **It's a cycle, not a straight line — evaluation often sends you back to scanning again.**

---

## 2. How Boncho Actually Decided to Add Delivery

* **She noticed something**: Rainy days meant fewer customers walking to her cart, but her friends kept asking if she delivered.
* **She thought it through**: Could she afford a delivery bag and a bit of extra staff time? Would nearby hostels actually order enough to be worth it?
* **She tried it, then watched closely**: She started with just two hostels, tracked orders weekly, and only expanded once it was clearly working.
* Without knowing it, Priya just walked through all four stages of the strategic management model.

---

## 3. Planning

* Looking outward and inward before making any big decision.
* **Outward**: What are customers, competitors, and the broader market doing?
* **Inward**: What can we actually afford, staff, and support well?
* **For Boncho**: Noticing rainy-day drop-offs and repeated delivery requests — that's scanning in action.

---

## 4. Strategy Formulation

* Designing the actual response — turning what you learned into a real choice.
* This is where you weigh options, consider trade-offs, and commit to one direction over the alternatives.
* **For Boncho**: Deciding to test delivery with just two nearby hostels first, instead of the whole neighbourhood at once.

---

## 5. Strategy Implementation

* Putting the plan into action — where most of the real work actually happens.
* This is where the policies, programs, procedures and budgets we learned last week come to life — the tools that make a strategy actually run day to day.
* **For Boncho**: Buying a delivery bag, telling regulars, and actually running orders for two weeks.

---

## 6. Evaluation & Control

* Checking whether it's actually working — and being willing to change course.
* Without this stage, a business keeps doing something long after it's stopped working. This stage often sends you straight back to scanning again.
* **For Broncho**: Tracking weekly delivery orders — and only expanding to more hostels once the numbers clearly justified it.

---

## 7. Her Delivery Decision, All Four Stages

* **Planning**: Noticed rain drop-offs and delivery requests.
* **Formulation**: Chose to test with 2 hostels, not the whole area.
* **Implementation**: Bought a delivery bag, ran it for 2 weeks.
* **Evaluation**: Tracked orders, then expanded once it worked.

---

## 9. Summary

* The strategic management model has four stages: **scan, formulate, implement, evaluate**.
* It's a cycle, not a straight line — evaluation often restarts the process.
* Even Priya's small delivery decision followed all four stages.
