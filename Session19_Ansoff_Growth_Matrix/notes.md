# Week 5 Session 19: Growth Strategies — The Ansoff Matrix

---

## 1. Core Concept: Two Questions, Four Answers

* **Igor Ansoff** published this matrix in a **1957 Harvard Business Review article**.
* It is nearly 70 years old and is still the first framework any manager reaches for when asked *“how do we grow?”*
* Are we selling an existing or a new product? To an existing or a new market? Two yes/no questions produce four growth strategies:

| Market \ Product | Existing Product | New Product |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Existing Market** | **Market Penetration** | **Product Development** |
| **New Market** | **Market Development** | **Diversification** |

---

## 2. Quadrant 1 of 4: Market Penetration (Sell More, Here)

* **Definition**: The safest growth strategy. Same product, same market, just capture more of it, through better pricing, more usage, or taking share from rivals.
* **Real-World Example (Colgate, ongoing)**:
  * Decades of campaigns encouraging Indians to brush twice a day rather than once.
  * Same toothpaste, same customers, just more frequent use — a textbook penetration play.
* **For Boncho’s Momos**:
  * A loyalty stamp card so regulars buy more often, without changing the menu or opening anywhere new.

---

## 3. Quadrant 2 of 4: Market Development (Same Product, New Customers)

* **Definition**: Take a product that already works and carry it to a market that hasn't seen it: a new city, a new state, a new country, or a new customer segment entirely.
* **Real-World Example (Haldiram's, 1990s onward)**:
  * A Bikaner sweets shop that carried the same bhujia and namkeen recipes first across North India, then nationally, then to Indian diaspora communities abroad, without changing the core product.
* **For Priya's Momos**:
  * Opening a second stall in a college campus across town — same menu, new customer base.

---

## 4. Quadrant 3 of 4: Product Development (New Product, Same Customers)

* **Definition**: Your existing customers already trust you. Give them a reason to spend more, with a genuinely new product built for the same relationship.
* **Real-World Example (Maggi, ongoing)**:
  * From one 2-minute noodle recipe to oats noodles, masala variants, and sauces, sold to the same households that already trusted the original brand.
* **For Boncho’s Momos**:
  * Adding a frozen, take-home pack of momos for customers who already love the fresh ones.

---

## 5. Quadrant 4 of 4: Diversification (New Product, New Market)

* **Definition**: The riskiest quadrant, and the only one where you have neither an existing product nor an existing customer base to fall back on.
* **Real-World Example (ITC, 1970s onward)**:
  * A cigarette company that is today also India's hotels business, one of its largest packaged foods companies, and a major paperboard manufacturer.
  * A new product, a new customer, almost every time.
* **For Boncho’s Momos**:
  * Opening a completely unrelated business, say, a laundry service, with nothing shared but the founder.

---

## 6. Core Concept: Rise in Risk

$$\text{Market Penetration (Lowest Risk)} \longrightarrow \text{Market Development} \longrightarrow \text{Product Development} \longrightarrow \text{Diversification (Highest Risk)}$$

* **Risk rises as you move away from what you know**.
* Neither product nor market is a known quantity in diversification.

---

## 7. From the Art of War: Sun Tzu, Chapter X

> *“Know the Ground on Which You Fight”*

* Sun Tzu, Chapter X, on terrain: a skilled general studies the ground before committing forces, because the same army can win easily on familiar terrain and be destroyed on unfamiliar terrain.
* **Strategic Parallel**: Market Penetration is fighting on home ground. Diversification is marching into terrain you've never seen.
* The Ansoff Matrix is, at heart, a map of how familiar your battlefield is.

## 8. Class Activity: Map Your Own Company

* **Group Task (4–5 members, 12 Minutes)**:
  1. In your group, pick one real Indian company everyone knows well.
  2. For each of the four quadrants, name one real or plausible move that company could make.
  3. Circle the one move you think is the smartest bet for that company right now, and be ready to defend it in one sentence.

### Debrief: What Did We Notice?
1. Which quadrant did most groups pick as the smartest bet, and why?
2. Did any group pick Diversification? What made it feel worth the risk?
3. For the companies discussed, which quadrant have they actually already tried, that you know of?

---

## 9. A Common Mistake: Chasing Diversification Too Soon

* Diversification looks exciting on a slide.
* In practice, most companies that skip Market Penetration and Development, and jump straight to a brand-new product in a brand-new market, are trying to escape a problem in their core business rather than genuinely pursuing an opportunity.
* **Rule**: Fix or understand the core first.

---

## 10. Key Takeaways from Today

* The Ansoff Matrix answers “how do we grow” with four options: **Penetration, Development (market or product), and Diversification**.
* Risk rises the further you move from your existing product and existing market.
* Market Penetration is the safest and usually the first move worth trying.
* Diversification is powerful but should follow real strategic logic, not be a way to avoid fixing a struggling core business.

> **How you grow matters as much as whether you grow.**
