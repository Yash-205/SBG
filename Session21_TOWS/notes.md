# Session 21: The TOWS Matrix — From Diagnosis to Prescription

---

## 1. SWOT vs. TOWS

| Framework | Format & Focus | Strategic Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SWOT** | A list. Four boxes of facts about your business and its environment. Good for understanding a situation. | **The Diagnosis** |
| **TOWS** | A matrix. Factors are paired into four strategy types. Good for deciding what to do next. | **The Prescription** |

> **SWOT tells you what's true; TOWS tells you what to do about it.**

---

## 2. Definition: What Is the TOWS Matrix?

* The **TOWS Matrix** pairs each external factor (Threats, Opportunities) with each internal factor (Weaknesses, Strengths) to generate four distinct sets of strategic options.
* **Origin**: Developed by **Heinz Weihrich (1982)** in *Long Range Planning* as a refinement of the classic SWOT framework.
* **Why the Inverted Name?**: The name deliberately flips the order — a reminder to **start from the outside (Threats, Opportunities)** before looking inside (Weaknesses, Strengths).

---

## 3. The Framework: The Four Strategy Types

| Strategy | Full Name | Pairing Logic | Strategic Posture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SO** | **Maxi-Maxi** | Use Strengths to capture Opportunities | **Attack / Offense** |
| **WO** | **Mini-Maxi** | Use Opportunities to overcome Weaknesses | **Fix & Grow** |
| **ST** | **Maxi-Mini** | Use Strengths to avoid or reduce Threats | **Defend** |
| **WT** | **Mini-Mini** | Minimize Weaknesses and avoid Threats | **Survive / Defense** |

---

## 4. Real-World Case Studies Across the Four Quadrants

### 1. SO Strategy (Maxi-Maxi) — Amul's Strength Meets Rising Demand
* **Strength**: Amul's cooperative network of 3.6 million farmers gives it the largest, most reliable milk-procurement chain in India.
* **Opportunity**: A fast-growing urban market for packaged dairy, ice cream, and value-added products.
* **SO Strategy**: Amul aggressively expanded into ice cream, cheese, and chocolate — using the exact same cooperative supply chain that built its milk business.

### 2. WO Strategy (Mini-Maxi) — Ola's EV Push Turns a Weakness Into a Fix
* **Weakness**: Ola's petrol-based cab fleet was expensive to fuel and hard to scale profitably at high volumes.
* **Opportunity**: Government EV subsidies (FAME II) and falling battery costs opened a path to cheaper, cleaner mobility.
* **WO Strategy**: Ola launched Ola Electric and pushed its fleet toward EVs — using the external opportunity to fix an internal cost weakness.

### 3. ST Strategy (Maxi-Mini) — Indian IT Blunts an Offshoring Threat
* **Strength**: A deep, English-speaking, cost-effective technical talent pool at firms like TCS, Infosys, and Wipro.
* **Threat**: Rising anti-outsourcing political sentiment in the US and Europe threatens offshore contracts.
* **ST Strategy**: Indian IT majors opened local delivery centres and hired directly in the US/UK — using their talent strength to blunt the threat, rather than retreating.

### 4. WT Strategy (Mini-Mini) — Jet Airways' Missing Prescription
* **Weakness**: High debt, an ageing fleet, and thin cash reserves.
* **Threat**: Aggressive low-cost rivals (IndiGo) and rising fuel costs squeezed margins further.
* **A WT Strategy Would Have Meant**: Selling non-core assets, cutting unprofitable routes, and finding a strategic partner early — defensive moves to survive, not grow. Jet delayed these too long and collapsed in 2019.

## 5. Running Example: Boncho’s Momos TOWS Matrix

### Boncho’s SWOT Analysis
* **Strength**: Loyal local customer base and a strong recipe reputation.
* **Weakness**: No personal delivery app presence; only a single physical outlet.
* **Opportunity**: Rising demand for late-night food delivery in her city.
* **Threat**: A new cloud-kitchen chain entering her neighbourhood.

### TOWS Pairings for Boncho’s Momos

## 6. Application: Which Cell Matters Most, and When?

*(Not a rigid thumb rule, but situational guidance)*

* **Growing business, strong position** $\longrightarrow$ Lean into **SO**
* **Growing business, weak position** $\longrightarrow$ Prioritize **WO**
* **Strong business, hostile environment** $\longrightarrow$ Prioritize **ST**
* **Weak business, hostile environment** $\longrightarrow$ **WT** is about survival, not growth

---

## 7. Caution: Limitations and Pitfalls

### Limitations of the TOWS Matrix
* **Garbage in, garbage out**: It is only as good as the SWOT feeding it.
* **Highly subjective**: Two teams can pair the same SWOT into very different strategies.
* **Does not prioritize**: It doesn't rank strategies — that requires portfolio tools like the BCG matrix.
* **Static snapshot**: Needs regular revisiting as internal and external factors shift.

### Common Pitfalls
* Writing strategies that just restate a SWOT item, instead of a real pairing.
* Filling all four cells equally instead of focusing where it matters most.
* Skipping SO/ST (offense) because WO/WT (defense) feels safer — or the reverse.
* Treating the matrix as a one-time exercise instead of revisiting it.

---

## 8. Process: Building a TOWS Matrix (Quick Exercise)

1. **Complete your SWOT list first**: TOWS only works with a solid SWOT underneath it.
2. **Draw a 2×2 grid**: Opportunities & Threats across the top, Strengths & Weaknesses down the side.
3. **Fill each cell by asking pairing questions**: e.g., *“How can we use this strength to capture this opportunity?”*
4. **Pick the 2–3 strongest strategies per cell**: Don't try to act on all of them at once.

## 9. Term of the Day: Potemkin Village

* **Definition**: Any construction (literal or figurative) built solely to deceive others into thinking that a situation is better than it really is.
* **Historical Origin**: Named after Grigory Potemkin, who allegedly erected fake settlements along the Dnieper River to impress Empress Catherine II during her visit to Crimea in 1787.
* **In Business**: Companies putting up superficial facades of success, technological capabilities, or financial health (such as inflated vanity metrics or fake partnerships) to attract investors and customers while the underlying business fundamentals remain hollow or broken.

---

## 10. Summary: Key Takeaways from Today

* SWOT tells you what's true; TOWS tells you what to do about it.
* Four strategy types: **SO (attack), WO (fix & grow), ST (defend), WT (survive)**.
* Always build TOWS from a solid SWOT — pairing quality depends on SWOT quality.
* The matrix doesn't prioritize for you — that's your job as a strategist.
* Real companies live in specific cells: *Amul in SO, Ola Electric in WO, Indian IT in ST, Jet Airways failed at WT*.
