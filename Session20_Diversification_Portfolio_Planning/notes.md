# Week 5 Session 20: Diversification & Introduction to Portfolio Planning

---

## 1. Core Concept: Why Most Diversification Actually Fails

1. **No Real Synergy**: Just a founder's personal interest in a new industry.
2. **Underestimating Competition**: Underestimating how different the new business's competitive dynamics really are.
3. **Resource Starvation**: Starving the new business of resources, or starving the old one to fund it.
4. **No Patience**: Expecting quick payback in a business that needs years to mature.

---

## 2. Related vs. Unrelated Diversification

| Type | Definition & Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Related Diversification** | The new business shares something real with the old one: a customer base, a distribution network, a brand, or a raw material. There is a genuine synergy to capture. |
| **Unrelated Diversification** | The new business shares nothing operational with the old one. The only connection is shared ownership and capital. Riskier, but sometimes the only real opportunity available. |

---

## 3. Case Story: ITC's Diversification Journey

### Part 1: From Tobacco to Adjacent Businesses (Related Diversification)
* **1910**: Founded as the Imperial Tobacco Company. For decades, cigarettes are essentially the entire business, and a formidable, high-margin one.
* **1970s–1990s**: Facing growing regulatory pressure on tobacco, ITC begins diversifying:
  * **Hotels**: Leveraging real estate and hospitality skills adjacent to its existing operations.
  * **Paperboards**: An input it already understood, since cigarette packaging needs paper.
* **Strategic Logic**: Both of these are **Related Diversification**, built on skills or inputs ITC already had a genuine claim to.

### Part 2: The FMCG Bet
* **2000s onward**: ITC launches into packaged foods and personal care: *Aashirvaad atta, Sunfeast biscuits, Bingo chips, Fiama, and Engage personal care*.
* A genuinely new customer relationship — daily household purchases, far from cigarettes.
* **The Synergy**: The synergy here wasn't a shared product; it was ITC's **existing distribution reach** into Indian retail, and its **financial strength** from cigarettes funding years of FMCG losses before the business turned profitable.

---

## 4. The Next Problem: Once You've Diversified

* ITC today runs cigarettes, hotels, paperboards, FMCG, and agri-business, all at once.
* Each needs a different amount of cash, attention, and patience.
* **The Portfolio Question**: How do you decide where to invest more, and where to stop investing?
* That's what **portfolio planning tools** exist to answer.

---

## 5. Core Concept: Introducing the BCG Matrix

* **Developed by the Boston Consulting Group in 1970**.
* Plots every business unit on two axes:
  1. **Market Growth Rate** (Vertical Axis): How fast is this market growing?
  2. **Relative Market Share** (Horizontal Axis): How strong is our share within it?
* Four resulting quadrants, each with a memorable name and a very different job to do for the company:

| Quadrant | Market Growth | Relative Market Share | Strategic Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **⭐ Stars** | High | High | Worth heavy investment; future drivers. |
| **❓ Question Marks** | High | Low | Expensive gambles; could become Stars or fail. |
| **🐄 Cash Cows** | Low | High | High cash generators; fund everything else. |
| **🐕 Dogs** | Low | Low | Low growth and weak share; candidates for retrenchment. |

---

## 6. Applying It: Mapping ITC's Own Businesses

| Business | BCG Quadrant | Strategic Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cigarettes** | **Cash Cow** | Mature market, still ITC's dominant share, funds everything else. |
| **FMCG (foods, personal care)** | **Question Mark $\rightarrow$ Star** | Growing fast; required years of investment before turning genuinely profitable. |
| **Hotels** | **Question Mark** | Cyclical, capital-heavy, real but uneven growth. |

---

## 7. From the Art of War: Sun Tzu, Chapter II

> *“The Treasury Runs Dry Near Home”*

* Sun Tzu warned that a state's treasury is drained not by the battle itself, but by supporting an army far from home for too long: transport, upkeep, and delay all bleed resources quietly.
* **Strategic Parallel**: A **Question Mark** business is exactly this — a campaign far from your home ground that can quietly drain the **Cash Cow** funding it if you don't set a real limit on patience.

## 8. Class Application: Tata Group Portfolio Mapping

| BCG Category | Tata Business / Company | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **⭐ Star** | **Tata Motors – EV business** | High market growth in electric vehicles + Tata has a strong and growing position in India's EV market. |
| **🐄 Cash Cow** | **Tata Consumer Products – Tea** | Mature tea market + strong brands such as Tata Tea generate steady cash flows. |
| **❓ Question Mark** | **Tata Digital / Tata Neu** | Digital commerce is a high-growth market, but Tata Neu faces strong competition from established players. |
| **🐕 Dog** | **Tata Teleservices (consumer mobile)** | Operates in a highly competitive/mature telecom environment and has historically had a relatively weak position. |

---

## 10. Key Takeaways from Today

* Related diversification shares a real synergy: customer base, distribution, or input; unrelated diversification shares only capital.
* ITC's journey shows both: hotels and paperboards were related moves; FMCG succeeded on distribution and financial synergy, not product overlap.
* Diversification usually fails from a lack of real synergy, underestimating the new business, or running out of patience.
* The BCG Matrix (Stars, Question Marks, Cash Cows, Dogs) is the tool for managing everything a diversified company owns.

> **A diversified company is a portfolio, not a single bet.**
