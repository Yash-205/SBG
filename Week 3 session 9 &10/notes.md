## 2. A Story to Start: The Phone That Ruled India, Then Nearly Vanished (Nokia)

* **Around 2009**: Nokia commands roughly 60% of India's mobile phone market — the default brand in nearly every pocket.
* **The Signal It Missed**: As Android-powered touchscreen smartphones began reshaping the market, Nokia stayed loyal to its own ageing software instead of reading where customers were clearly heading.
* **A Few Years Later**: Nokia's share collapsed as customers moved to Android phones — including budget Indian brands — and its handset business was eventually sold off entirely.

> *History based on widely documented accounts of Nokia's decline in India and globally (post-2009).*

---

## 3. What Is Environmental Scanning?

* The ongoing habit of watching for signals of change in customers, competitors, technology, and the wider world before those changes force your hand.

### Nokia's Signal Was There for Years

* Touchscreens, app ecosystems, and Android were all visible well before Nokia's market share actually collapsed.
* Scanning isn't about predicting the unpredictable — it's about actually noticing what's already visible.

---

## 4. Two Directions to Look: Scanning Outward vs. Scanning Inward

> **Real scanning always looks both ways at once.**

| Outward Scanning                                      | Inward Scanning                                              |
| :---------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| What are customers, competitors and technology doing? | What can we actually afford, staff and support well?         |
| *Nokia should have watched Android's rise closely*  | *Nokia should have honestly assessed its own software gap* |

---

## 5. What Is Forecasting?

* Turning the signals you've scanned into an actual, usable guess about the future.
* Scanning without forecasting just leaves you with a pile of interesting facts. Forecasting forces you to commit to what those facts likely mean for tomorrow.
* No forecast is ever perfectly right — the goal is a reasoned, honest guess, not a guarantee.

---

## 6.Three Ways to Forecast

1. **Trend Extrapolation**: If a pattern has held for years, assume it broadly continues — unless you see a real reason it won't.
2. **Expert Judgment**: Ask people who've watched this space for years what they honestly expect.
3. **Scenario Thinking**: Sketch a best case, a worst case, and a most-likely case — then plan for more than one.

---

## 7. Back to Nokia: What Real Scanning Would Have Shown

* **Outward Signal Missed**: Android adoption climbing fast among developers and rival phone makers.
* **Inward Signal Ignored**: Its own software (Symbian) was falling further behind every year.
* **A Better Forecast**: *“If this trend continues, touchscreens and app stores become the default within a few years.”*

---

## 8. Back to Priya's Momos: Scanning Her Own Market

* **Outward**: Two new food-delivery apps have started covering her college's neighbourhood this year.
* **Inward**: She's never tracked how many of her customers actually order through those apps versus walking up to her cart.
* **Her Forecast**: If delivery apps keep growing, cart-only sales may plateau within a year — worth watching closely, not ignoring.

> *“Priya's Momos” is a fictional business created for classroom illustration only.*

## 9.What Signals Are You Missing?

Think about a business you know well — a family shop, a college canteen, anything:

* What outward signal might they be ignoring right now?
* What inward weakness might they be avoiding looking at honestly?
* If current trends continue for 2 more years, what happens to them?

---

## 10. Why This Matters for a Founder

1. Small businesses can't afford Nokia's mistake — there's no cushion of size to survive it.
2. Scanning is cheap; ignoring visible change is expensive later.
3. A simple weekly habit of asking “what changed this week?” beats an annual deep-dive nobody does.

---

## 11. Key Takeaways from Today

* Environmental scanning means watching for signals of change before they force your hand.
* Scan both outward (the world) and inward (your own capabilities) at once.
* Forecasting turns scanned signals into a usable, honest guess about what's coming.
* Nokia's signals were visible for years — the failure was in not acting on them.

---

## 12. Term of the Day: Fabian Strategy & Gray-Zone Warfare

* **Fabian Strategy**: Where a side avoids direct, large battles and instead wears down an enemy through a slow war of attrition, minor skirmishes, and supply disruption.
* **Gray-Zone Warfare**: Operating in the ambiguous space between direct confrontation and peace, gradually altering conditions without triggering an all-out retaliation.
