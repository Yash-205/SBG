# Week 1 Session 2: Strategies for Business Growth — Policy, Program & Procedure

---

## 1. RECAP

* Strategy is deliberate direction, not just hard work.
* Haldiram's grew from one Bikaner shop through deliberate choices over decades.

---

## 2. What Is Strategic Management?

* **Decision-Making**: Choosing among alternatives, deliberately.
* **Planning**: Deciding today what to do to reach tomorrow's goal.
* **Formulation**: Designing the strategy itself.
* **Evaluation**: Checking whether the strategy is working.

---

## 3. Why Every Haldiram's Outlet Feels the Same

Walk into a Haldiram's in Delhi, then one in Nagpur. The soan papdi tastes the same. The counters look the same. Nobody is standing over every employee, every day, making sure of that. Something else is doing that work quietly, in the background.

That's what a policy does — and it's where we start today.

> *Illustrative framing based on Haldiram's publicly documented multi-city operations.*

---

## 4. What Is a Policy?

* A standing guideline for how decisions get made — written once, applied again and again.
* A policy doesn't tell you exactly what to do in every situation; it tells you the boundary you must stay inside while deciding.

### Example

> *“We only use fresh ingredients, never leftovers from the previous day”* — a rule every future decision must respect.

---

## 5. What Is a Program?

* A set of activities designed to accomplish a specific goal, usually with a start and an end.
* Unlike a policy, a program isn't a permanent guideline; it's a temporary push toward one particular objective.

### Example

* A *“weekend combo offer”* running for two months to build repeat weekend customers — a defined activity with a defined end.

---

## 6. What Is a Procedure?

* A step-by-step method for carrying out a policy or activity, in the same order, every time.
* If a policy is the boundary, a procedure is the exact path someone walks inside it — no guesswork, no room for the day's mood.

### Example

* Wash hands → steam the momos → plate them → add chutney → serve — the same five steps, every single plate.

---

## 7. Putting Them Together: Policy vs. Program vs. Procedure

| Policy                           | Program                      | Procedure                         |
| :------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| Standing guideline               | Time-bound set of activities | Step-by-step method               |
| Lasts indefinitely               | Has a start and an end       | Repeats identically every time    |
| *“Always fresh ingredients”* | *“Weekend combo offer”*  | *“Five steps to plate momos”* |

---

## 8. His First Policy

> *“We use only fresh vegetables, prepared the same day — nothing from yesterday goes into a plate.”*

Notice what this policy does: it doesn't tell her staff exactly what to cook today. It just sets a boundary every future decision has to respect, even as she hires new staff or opens new carts.

---

## 9. Her First Program & Procedure

* **Program**: *“Friday Combo Week”* — 6 plates for the price of 5, every Friday for the next 8 weeks, to build a weekend habit.
* **Procedure**: Wash hands → steam for exactly 8 minutes → plate with tongs → add one chutney sachet → hand over with a smile — every single order, every single time.

---

## 10. Class Discussion: Spot the Term

For each statement, call out: is it a policy, a program, or a procedure?

1. *“All customer complaints must be resolved within 24 hours.”*
   **Answer**: **Policy** (A standing guideline that sets a boundary for ongoing decisions).
2. *“Our 3-day flash sale for college festival week.”*
   **Answer**: **Program** (A time-bound push with a defined start and end).
3. *“Every new employee shadows a senior staff member for their first three shifts.”*
   **Answer**: **Procedure** (A step-by-step repeatable method).

---

## 11. A Word of Caution: Why Entrepreneurs Skip This and Regret It Later

* At one cart, Priya doesn't need a written policy — she remembers everything herself. But the moment she hires her first employee, or opens a second cart, memory stops being enough.
* Businesses that skip writing these down don't fail immediately — they fail quietly, through inconsistency, the moment they try to grow.

---

## 12. Summary: Key Takeaways from Today

* A policy is a standing guideline; it sets a boundary, not a script.
* A program is a time-bound set of activities aimed at one goal.
* A procedure is a fixed, repeatable, step-by-step method.
* Writing these down matters most exactly when a business starts to grow beyond one person.
