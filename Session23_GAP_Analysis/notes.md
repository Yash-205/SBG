# Week 6 Session 23: GAP Analysis — How Much Growth Do You Actually Need?

---

## 1. Core Concept: What Is GAP Analysis?

* **Definition**: A GAP Analysis measures the distance between where a business *wants* to be (its strategic objectives) and where it is *projected* to be if it continues on its current trajectory without major strategic changes.
* **Origin**: Introduced by **Igor Ansoff (1965)** in his seminal book *Corporate Strategy*.
* **Core Principle**: The gap represents the **growth deficit that strategy must actively close**.

```
Performance /
Revenue
   ^                                      [ Desired Growth Objective ]
   |                                                  /
   |                                                 /
   |                                                /  <--- STRATEGIC GAP
   |                                               /        (Strategy must close this)
   |                                              /
   |                               [ Current Trajectory / Projection ]
   |                                             /
   |                                            /
   +------------------------------------------------------------> Time
```

---

## 2. Two Types of Gaps

| Gap Type | Description | How It Is Solved |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operating Gap** | The distance between current results and what the current business could achieve if fully optimized. | Fixed through internal efficiency, cost cutting, and operational improvements. |
| **Strategic Gap** | The distance between the fully optimized current business and the higher growth objective. | Requires new strategic moves: new products, new markets, acquisitions, or diversification. |

---

## 3. Closing the Gap: The Four Strategic Routes (Ansoff Framework)

1. **Intensive Growth**: Growing within current business domain (Market Penetration, Market Development, Product Development).
2. **Integrative Growth**: Growing by controlling links in the value chain or industry:
   * *Backward Integration*: Moving into suppliers / raw materials.
   * *Forward Integration*: Moving into distribution / retail.
   * *Horizontal Integration*: Acquiring or merging with direct competitors.
3. **Diversification Growth**: Adding new businesses outside current operations (Related or Unrelated).
4. **Combination**: Pursuing multiple routes simultaneously.

---

## 4. Real-World Case Studies

### 1. Case: Airtel's Multi-Route Strategic Gap (2016–2019)
* **The Gap**: Reliance Jio's entry in 2016 with free voice and cheap data created a massive, existential revenue and subscriber gap for Airtel.
* **The Multi-Route Response**:
  * **Intensive**: Aggressive 4G data pack bundling and nationwide network upgrades to defend subscriber share.
  * **Integrative (Horizontal)**: Acquired the wireless operations of Telenor India and Tata Teleservices to instantly absorb spectrum and customers.
  * **Diversification / Value-Added**: Expanded Airtel Payments Bank, Wynk Music, and Airtel Xstream to deepen digital stickiness.
* **The Lesson**: Large strategic gaps rarely get closed by one move alone — Airtel combined intensive, integrative, and diversification strategies simultaneously.

### 2. Case: Vodafone-Idea (Horizontal Integration for Survival)
* **The Gap**: Both Vodafone India and Idea Cellular were individually losing market share and cash rapidly after Jio's price war began.
* **The Response**: The 2018 merger combined their subscriber bases, spectrum, and infrastructure — horizontal integration aimed at regaining the scale neither could reach alone.
* **The Lesson**: Not every gap gets closed by growth — sometimes closing a gap means merging just to survive.

## 5. Running Example: Boncho’s Momos — Closing Her Growth Gap

* **Her Growth Gap**: Boncho wants to double her monthly revenue within a year. But if she changes nothing, her single outlet's steady regulars will only grow revenue by a small amount — that shortfall is her gap.
* **Choosing Her Closing Strategy**:
  * Boncho’s gap is mostly **strategic, not operating** — her single outlet is already running efficiently, so trimming costs alone won't double her revenue.
  * **Her Best Bet**: Combine **product development** (her fusion menu) with a **delivery partnership** (extending reach without owning logistics) — two routes at once, just like Airtel did.

---

## 6. Process: Building a GAP Analysis (Step-by-Step)

1. **Set a Clear Growth Objective**: A specific, quantified number with a deadline, not a vague ambition.
2. **Forecast Current Trajectory**: Project where the business will land if nothing changes.
3. **Measure the Gap**: Calculate the exact difference between the objective and the projection over time.
4. **Choose One or More Closing Strategies**: Select from intensive, integrative, diversification, or combination approaches.

---

## 7. Caution: Limitations and Pitfalls

### Limitations of GAP Analysis
* It depends entirely on how realistic your “projected” line actually is.
* A large gap doesn't automatically tell you which closing strategy will actually work.
* It is a planning tool, not an execution plan — closing the gap still takes real strategic choices.
* External shocks can move both lines unpredictably, making the gap a moving target.

### Common Pitfalls
* Setting an objective that is aspirational but has no real link to the business's capabilities.
* Projecting the current trajectory too optimistically, which understates the real gap.
* Picking a closing strategy because it is familiar, not because it fits the specific gap.
* Treating the gap as closed once a plan exists, without tracking actual progress.

---

## 8. Summary: Key Takeaways from Today

* GAP Analysis measures the **distance between your growth objective and your current trajectory**.
* The gap is what strategy actually has to close — not just a vague desire to “grow more.”
* **Closing routes**: Intensive, Integrative, Diversification, or a Combination of these.
* Real companies often close gaps with more than one route at once — as Airtel did after Jio's disruption.
