# Week 2 Session 8: Levels of Strategy — Corporate, SBU & Functional

---

## 1. One Group, Many Businesses, Different Questions (Tata Group & Titan)

* **The Top of the House**: Tata Group decides which industries to be in at all — steel, cars, software, watches and jewellery through Titan, and many more.
* **Inside One Business**: Titan itself then decides how to actually win inside the watch and jewellery markets — against HMT, against family jewellers.
* **Inside One Department**: Titan's marketing team decides how to advertise; its operations team decides how to run factories — each supporting the same fight, from a different angle.

---

## 2. What Is Corporate-Level Strategy?

* **The Question**: *Which businesses should we even be in?*
* Decided by the very top of an organization — whether to enter, exit, or expand into entirely different industries.

### Example

* Tata Group's decision to be in steel, cars, IT services, and consumer lifestyle products all at once, under one umbrella.

---

## 3. What Is SBU-Level Strategy?

* **The Question**: *How do we actually win inside this one business?*
* **SBU** stands for Strategic Business Unit — one distinct business within a larger group, competing in its own specific market.

### Example

* Titan deciding how to compete on trust and design against traditional family jewellers — a question specific to the jewellery business, not Tata Group as a whole.

---

## 4. What Is Functional-Level Strategy?

* **The Question**: *How does each department support that win, day to day?*
* The narrowest level — marketing, operations, finance and HR each make their own choices that ladder up to the SBU's strategy.

### Example

* Titan's marketing team choosing which festival campaigns to run; its operations team deciding how to staff each store — functional choices, in service of the bigger fight.

---

## 5. Three Levels, Side by Side

| Corporate                     | SBU                            | Functional                  |
| :---------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| Which businesses to be in     | How to win in one business     | How each department helps   |
| Tata Group's portfolio choice | Titan vs. family jewellers     | Titan's marketing campaigns |
| Decided by top leadership     | Decided by business-unit heads | Decided by department heads |

---

## 6. Back to Titan: SBU-Level Decisions Inside a Corporate Umbrella

* **Tata Group's Corporate Decision**: Be in watches and jewellery at all, through Titan.
* **Titan's SBU-Level Decisions Within That**: Introducing certified purity and transparent pricing to win customer trust away from generations-old family jewellers — a choice specific to competing in jewellery, made by the people running that business, not by Tata Group's top leadership directly.

---

## 7. Do Small Businesses Have Levels Too?

* **“Corporate”**: Should Boncho open a second cart, or add a delivery-only kitchen?
* **“SBU”**: How does the momo cart itself compete on taste and price?
* **“Functional”**: How does she plan ingredient purchases and staff shifts?

> *Even a one-person business makes decisions at all three levels — just without the fancy titles.*
> *“Priya's Momos” is a fictional business created for classroom illustration only.*

---

## 8. Class Discussion: Which Level Made This Decision?

1. *“We are shutting down our least profitable business unit entirely.”* $\rightarrow$ **Corporate**
2. *“Our sales team will start following up with customers within 24 hours.”* $\rightarrow$ **Functional**
3. *“We will now offer both a budget and a premium version of our product.”* $\rightarrow$ **SBU / Business**

---

## 9. Why Founders Need to Know Which Level They're At

1. A corporate-level mistake (entering the wrong business) is far more expensive to undo than a functional one.
2. Investors and co-founders often disagree because they're arguing about different levels without realizing it.
3. As your business grows past one person, someone has to own each level clearly.

---

## 10. Summary:

* Corporate-level strategy decides which businesses to be in at all.
* SBU-level strategy decides how to win inside one specific business.
* Functional-level strategy decides how each department supports that win.
* Even a single small business makes decisions at all three levels.

---

## 11. Review & Connection: The Four Stages of the Strategic Management Model

1. **Planning**: Look outward and inward before deciding anything.
2. **Formulation**: Design the actual strategy.
3. **Implementation**: Put the strategy into action.
4. **Evaluation**: Check if it's working, and adjust.

* *It's a cycle, not a straight line — evaluation often sends you back to scanning again.*

---

## 12. Term of the Day: Schwerpunkt & Achilles' Heel

* **Schwerpunkt**: Focal Point or Center of Gravity.
* **Achilles' Heel**: Similarly, all organizations have an Achilles heel.
